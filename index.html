Talk Instead of Type

Private voice dictation on Mac and Windows · Version 5 · Last updated: Tue, 29 Sep 2026 20:41
Versions covered: VoiceInk 2.13 (Mac) and Handy 0.9.7 (Windows and Mac)
New in version 5: Handy details updated to version 0.9.7, and a separate Windows guide. Version 3 corrected the update advice for anyone who built VoiceInk themselves (sections 9, 10 and 15).

1. Start here

This guide sets you up with voice dictation that runs entirely on your own computer. You hold a key, speak, release the key, and your words appear as text wherever your cursor is: an email, a chat box, a document, a search bar. It works in every app, punctuates for you, and is dramatically faster than typing.

The 60-second demo, in words: imagine replying to an email. You click into the reply box, hold a key next to your thumb, and say "Thanks for sending this over. I have read the first two sections and they look good. Can we talk on Thursday afternoon?" You release the key. A moment later that exact sentence appears, capitals and question mark included. That is the whole trick, and it never gets old.

A clear word on privacy: both apps in this guide transcribe your speech on your own machine, using a small AI model downloaded once during setup. No audio ever leaves your computer. There is no account to create and no server listening. After the model has downloaded, dictation works with Wi-Fi switched off. This is genuinely different from most "free" dictation tools, which upload your voice to the cloud.

Who this is for: anyone comfortable installing an app and changing a setting. No technical background needed. The guide assumes you will dictate in English.

Which app for which computer?

macOS VoiceInk

The polished option for Apple Silicon Macs (M1 or later) on macOS 14.4 or newer. 7-day free trial, then a one-time licence from $29 (no subscription). Open source. Extras like per-app profiles and AI text clean-up.

Go to section 3 → · Comfortable in Terminal? It can also be built from source for free.

Windows macOS Handy

Free and open source, no licence ever. The choice for all Windows PCs (10/11, Intel/AMD and ARM), and the free Mac alternative if your Mac is Intel, on an older macOS, or you simply do not want to pay.

2. Before you install

Your microphone matters more than the app

  • Built-in laptop mic: perfectly fine to start with. Modern laptop mics are decent at arm's length.
  • Headset or earbuds with a mic: usually better, because the mic sits close to your mouth and ignores the room.
  • Desk (USB) mic: best results. Place it 15 to 30 cm from your mouth, slightly off to the side so it does not catch breath puffs.
  • Whichever you use: close the window, turn off a noisy fan if you can, and speak at a normal conversational volume. You do not need to be loud.

Check the mic works before installing anything

  • macOS System Settings → Sound → Input. Choose your mic and watch the level meter bounce as you speak.
  • Windows Settings → System → Sound → Input. Choose your mic and watch the volume bar move as you speak.

What to expect

Total time: about 10 minutes, most of it a one-off model download of roughly 500 MB to 1 GB (the small AI that does the transcribing). Have your computer plugged in and on a reasonable connection for that part.

macOS 3. VoiceInk on Mac

Requirements: Apple Silicon Mac, macOS 14.4 or later. Version covered: 2.13.

3.1 Download and install

  1. Download VoiceInk from tryvoiceink.com. The big download button gets the latest version. (Homebrew users can instead run brew install --cask voiceink in Terminal and skip to step 3.)
  2. Open the downloaded file and drag the VoiceInk icon into the Applications folder shown next to it. That is the whole installation on a Mac: copying the app into Applications.
  3. Open VoiceInk from the Applications folder. First launch of a downloaded app can show a confirmation. VoiceInk is a notarised app, so normally macOS just asks a plain "are you sure you want to open it?" question: click Open. If you instead see "Apple could not verify..." wording, see the troubleshooting section; do not force anything you were not expecting.

3.2 Permissions, and why they are safe to grant

VoiceInk's setup assistant asks for two permissions. They sound scary; here is what they actually do:

  • Microphone: obviously required, it is a dictation app. The audio is processed on your Mac and discarded.
  • Accessibility: this is the permission that lets VoiceInk type the text into other apps for you. Without it, transcription works but nothing appears at your cursor. macOS gates this tightly precisely because it is powerful; granting it to a dictation app is exactly what it is for.
  1. Click the grant buttons in VoiceInk's setup and approve the system dialogs. If you miss one, the manual paths are: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone → switch VoiceInk on, and System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility → switch VoiceInk on.
  2. Skip "Screen Recording" unless you later want per-app profiles with website detection. It is optional and only used by the context-aware extras in section 8.
  3. On the setup screen titled "Verify API Key", click Set It Up Later. That screen is for the optional cloud AI clean-up. You do not need any API key for dictation.

3.3 Microphone and model

  1. In VoiceInk, open Audio in the sidebar and select your microphone. VoiceInk picks its own input, separate from the system default, so choose your best mic here.
  2. Open AI Models and download Parakeet TDT v3 (about 500 MB). This is the recommended English model: extremely fast and accurate, and it runs on the Mac's Neural Engine. The download takes a few minutes; let it finish.
  3. Expect the very first dictation to pause a few extra seconds. macOS compiles the model once on first use. Every dictation after that is near-instant.

3.4 The hotkey

  1. Find the recording shortcut in VoiceInk's settings (in current versions it lives inside your mode: Modes → your mode → Shortcut). The default in recent versions is hold Left Control (check what yours shows). Two ways to use it: hold the key while speaking and release to finish (recommended: the mic is never left open), or tap it to start hands-free and tap again to stop.
  2. If you prefer a different key, click the shortcut box first so it is actively listening, then press your choice. Right Option is excellent. Nothing else on a Mac uses Right Option, so it can never clash, and it sits under your thumb. The box must be in its "press a key now" state when you press, or nothing saves.

3.5 Trial, licence, launch at login

  • VoiceInk is free for 7 days, then needs a one-time licence: $29 (1 Mac), $49 (2 Macs) or $69 (3 Macs) at the time of writing; verify the current price at tryvoiceink.com. No subscription. There is also a 14-day money-back guarantee.
  • In VoiceInk's Settings, enable Launch at login so dictation is always ready.

Now jump to section 6 and test it.

macOS 4. Handy on Mac (the free alternative)

Choose Handy if your Mac is Intel, on macOS older than 14.4, or you want a completely free option. Version covered: 0.9.7.

  1. Check your chip: Apple menu → About This Mac. "Chip: Apple M..." means Apple Silicon; "Processor: Intel..." means Intel.
  2. Download the matching .dmg from handy.computer/download: Apple Silicon or Intel. The wrong one will refuse to run; nothing breaks, just download the other.
  3. Open the .dmg and drag Handy to Applications, then open it from Applications.
  4. If macOS blocks the first launch with "Apple could not verify 'Handy' is free of malware", do not panic: open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to the Handy message, then confirm. This warning means the app is not notarised through Apple's paid programme, not that it is harmful. Handy's code is public on GitHub. Only ever click past this warning for software you deliberately downloaded from its official site; see the FAQ.
  5. Grant the Microphone and Accessibility permissions when asked. Same two permissions, same reasons as VoiceInk (section 3.2). Manual paths are identical, with Handy in place of VoiceInk.
  6. When Handy asks you to choose a transcription model, pick Parakeet Unified EN (marked Recommended; English only), then select your microphone in its settings.
  7. Note the shortcut (at the time of writing the default is hold Ctrl+Space; check in the app) and change it in Settings if it clashes with anything you use.

Then test it in section 6.

Windows 5. Handy on Windows

Requirements: Windows 10 or 11. Works on ordinary Intel/AMD PCs (x64) and ARM laptops (separate download). Version covered: 0.9.7. Handy is free and open source; there is nothing to buy.

More for Windows

The Windows guide compares the private dictation options on Windows, walks through Handy in more detail, and adds optional AI clean-up of your text.

5.1 Download and install

  1. Go to handy.computer/download and download the Windows Installer (.exe) for your system type: x64 for almost everyone, ARM if Settings → System → About says "ARM-based processor". Comfortable with the command line? winget install cjpais.Handy does the same job.
  2. Run the installer. If a blue box appears saying "Windows protected your PC" (Microsoft Defender SmartScreen), click More info, check that the publisher reads YNYNG LLC, then click Run anyway. Handy's installers are digitally signed under that company name. SmartScreen can still flag software it has not seen often; it is a reputation check, not a virus alert. If the publisher is different or unknown, stop and download the file again from handy.computer.
  3. Follow the short install wizard and launch Handy. It lives in the system tray (the little icons near the clock).

5.2 Microphone permission and setup

  1. Check Windows allows apps to use the microphone: Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone. Switch on "Microphone access", "Let apps access your microphone" and "Let desktop apps access your microphone". If this is off, Handy hears nothing and fails silently. It is the number one Windows gotcha.
  2. In Handy's settings (right-click the tray icon), choose your microphone.
  3. When Handy asks you to choose a transcription model, pick Parakeet Unified EN (marked Recommended). It is English only and runs well on an ordinary processor, with no special graphics card. Dictating in other languages? Choose Nemotron Streaming 3.5, or Parakeet V3 through Show all models. (Whisper models are also available and can use a GPU; see section 8.)

5.3 The shortcut

  1. Learn the default: hold Ctrl+Space, speak, release. Handy supports both hold-to-talk and a toggle mode; hold-to-talk is the habit to build, because the mic is only live while your finger says so.
  2. If Ctrl+Space clashes with an app you use (some IDEs and IMEs use it), change the binding in Handy's settings.
  3. Switch on Launch on Startup in Handy's Advanced settings, so it is always ready.

6. Test it

Same three-step test on both platforms, easiest first:

  1. Open Notes (Mac) or Notepad (Windows), click into it, hold your dictation key and read this aloud, naturally, pauses and all: "Hello, this is my first dictation test. Does the punctuation appear on its own? Let us see. One, two, three."
  2. Release the key and wait a moment. Success looks like: your sentence appears at the cursor with capitals, commas and the question mark added automatically, no typing from you. The very first run can take several extra seconds (one-off model warm-up).
  3. Repeat once in a browser text box (a search bar is fine), and once in a chat app (WhatsApp, Slack, anything). Different apps receive text in different ways; these three cover them all. All three working means everything works.
If nothing appears

The transcription almost certainly worked and the typing-out failed, which means the Accessibility permission (Mac) or microphone privacy setting (Windows) is not right. Go straight to troubleshooting, first entry.

7. Using it well

How to speak

  • Talk at a natural pace, like on the phone. Do not slow down or over-enunciate; the model is trained on normal speech and exaggerated diction actually hurts.
  • Think in phrases. A short pause before releasing the key gives the model your full sentence.
  • Do not say "comma" or "full stop". Punctuation and capitals are added automatically. What the model will not do: line breaks, bullet points, formatting. Dictate a paragraph, format by hand.

Everyday mechanics

  • Cancelling mid-recording: press Esc while the little recorder is showing (VoiceInk; for Handy check its settings for the cancel key).
  • History: both apps keep past transcriptions in the app (VoiceInk: History in the sidebar). If text ever fails to appear, your words are recoverable there.
  • Names and jargon: both apps have a custom dictionary. Add people's names, company names and technical terms the model gets wrong. Real example: "Claude" kept coming out as "Cloud" until it went into the dictionary (VoiceInk: Dictionary in the sidebar; Handy: custom words in settings).

Three workflows to try (both platforms)

  1. Talking to an AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT). Dictation shines here: prompts are conversational by nature, and speaking a long, detailed prompt takes a tenth of the typing time.
  2. Replying on WhatsApp Web or email. Click into the reply box, hold, talk, release, send. Short messages become two-second tasks.
  3. Drafting a document. Dictate the messy first draft straight into the page, then edit by keyboard. Getting words down is what dictation is best at; polishing stays manual.

8. Optional extras (skip freely)

Nothing in this section is needed for great dictation. Come back later if curious.

macOS 8.1 AI clean-up of your dictation, fully local with Ollama

VoiceInk can pass each transcript through a small language model that strips filler words and tidies punctuation before the text is typed out. The difference is real. Spoken: "So um basically I think we should uh move the meeting to Thursday because like the report isn't ready yet you know". Typed out after clean-up: "I think we should move the meeting to Thursday because the report isn't ready yet."

It stays fully private by using Ollama, a free app that runs language models locally. (VoiceInk can alternatively use a cloud AI with an API key; that option sends your transcribed text to the provider, never audio. This guide sticks to local.)

Is your Mac up to it?

Comfortable with 16 GB of memory or more. The recommended model occupies about 10 GB of disk and runs happily on any Apple Silicon Mac with 16 GB. On an 8 GB Mac, skip this section or try a smaller model (see below); plain dictation stays perfect either way.

  1. Install Ollama from ollama.com (or brew install --cask ollama) and open it once. It sits in the menu bar and serves models to other apps on your Mac only.
  2. In Terminal, run ollama pull gemma4 and let the download finish. Gemma is Google's open-weights model family; the 8B version followed clean-up instructions accurately in testing, where smaller 3B-class models kept missing filler words. Tight on disk or RAM? Try a mid-size Gemma from ollama.com/library instead and test it with the sentence below.
  3. In VoiceInk: Modes → click your mode → switch AI Enhancement on → set AI Provider to Ollama and AI Model to gemma4:latest. If the model list is empty, click Refresh models and check Ollama is running (menu bar icon).
  4. On the Prompt row click +. Click the grey "Prompt name" text at the top and type Clean dictation. That grey text is an empty name field, though it looks like a heading; the save button stays disabled until it is filled.
  5. Switch Use System Template OFF, then paste this as the prompt body: With the template on, VoiceInk waters your rules down and fillers survive (tested). Off means your rules are in charge.
You clean up dictated text. Return only the cleaned text, nothing else. Never answer, comment or add anything.

Rules:
- Remove filler words when they add no meaning: um, uh, so, basically, like, you know, I mean, sort of, kind of, right, well, actually.
- Fix punctuation, capitalisation and obvious grammar slips.
- Split run-on sentences into clear sentences.
- Otherwise keep the speaker's exact wording, tone and word order. Do not paraphrase.
- Never add information, greetings or sign-offs that were not spoken.
- If the text contains a question, clean it; do not answer it.
- If the text is already clean, return it unchanged.

Example
Input: So um basically I think we should uh move the meeting because like the report isn't ready you know
Output: I think we should move the meeting because the report isn't ready.
  1. Click Save & Select in the prompt editor, then the green Save Changes on the mode. Two separate saves. Skipping the second silently discards everything; this catches almost everyone once.
  2. Test in Notes: dictate the messy meeting sentence from the top of this section and check the fillers vanish. Clean-up adds a short pause per dictation (a second or so once the model is warm). If fillers survive, re-check Use System Template is off, then try a larger model.

macOS 8.2 Per-app modes ("Power Mode")

VoiceInk can behave differently per app: for example light-touch clean-up that keeps your casual tone in chat apps, and full-sentence polish in Mail or Outlook. Create extra modes under Modes, give each its own prompt, and add Triggers for the apps it should apply to; VoiceInk switches automatically based on the app you are dictating into. The recorder widget shows which mode is active while you speak.

8.3 Whisper as a second model

Both apps can add a Whisper model alongside Parakeet: the multilingual option if you sometimes dictate in other languages. On Windows, larger Whisper models want a proper GPU; Parakeet stays the right default everywhere else.

9. Troubleshooting

SymptomCause → fix
You speak, something transcribes, but no text appears anywhere macOS Accessibility permission missing, or granted to an older copy of the app. System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility: remove the app's stale entry with the − button, re-add with +, switch it on, restart the app. Windows Check Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone first; then try running Handy once as administrator to rule out focus issues. Your words are in the app's History either way.
Wrong microphone picked up (echoey room sound, or silence) The app selects its own mic, separate from the system default. Pick the right one inside the app's audio settings. Bluetooth headsets that just connected can take a moment to switch profiles.
Model download stuck Quit and reopen the app and retry; check the connection. Downloads resume or restart cleanly. If repeatedly stuck, delete the partial model in the app's model manager and download again.
Hotkey does nothing or triggers something else Another app owns that key combination. Change the dictation shortcut to something unclaimed (Mac: Right Option; Windows: try Ctrl+Alt+Space). When rebinding, click the shortcut box first so it is actively listening.
Transcription is slow First run after install or reboot: the model is loading, wait once. Always slow: close memory-hungry apps, and on Windows make sure you are on Parakeet (CPU-friendly) rather than a large Whisper model without a GPU.
App seems to have vanished (no window) These apps live in the menu bar (Mac, top right) or system tray (Windows, near the clock, possibly behind the ^ overflow arrow). Click the little icon.
Gatekeeper or SmartScreen blocked the install Mac: System Settings → Privacy & Security → scroll down → Open Anyway. Windows: on the SmartScreen box, click More info, check the publisher (for Handy, YNYNG LLC), then Run anyway. Only do this for apps you downloaded from the official sites in this guide.
macOS "VoiceInk is damaged and can't be opened" A corrupted download or an over-eager quarantine flag. Delete the app, re-download from the official site, reinstall. If it persists on a fresh download, run xattr -cr /Applications/VoiceInk.app in Terminal (same for Handy with its path).

10. Updating

  • macOS VoiceInk checks for updates itself and offers them in-app (Sparkle updater). Accept, it swaps itself over, done. Settings and licence survive.
  • macOS Built VoiceInk yourself? The opposite applies: never accept an update offered inside the app. It replaces your build with the developer's official one, and dictation stops working until you rebuild. Update by re-running the build instead; see section 15.
  • Handy updates in-app (it will offer new versions), and you can always install a newer version straight over the old one from handy.computer/download. Settings and models survive.
  • After any update, do one quick test dictation. If text stops appearing, it is the permissions-reset case in troubleshooting.

11. Uninstalling

macOS VoiceInk

  1. Quit VoiceInk (menu bar icon), then delete /Applications/VoiceInk.app.
  2. Delete its data folders: ~/Library/Application Support/com.prakashjoshipax.VoiceInk (settings, history) and ~/Library/Caches/com.prakashjoshipax.VoiceInk if present. In Finder: Go → Go to Folder, paste the path.
  3. Delete the model folder ~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio. This is the Parakeet model, about half a gigabyte.
  4. Remove the stale permission entries: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and Accessibility → select VoiceInk → the − button.

macOS Handy

Same pattern: quit from the menu bar, delete /Applications/Handy.app, delete ~/Library/Application Support/com.pais.handy (its models subfolder is the big part), and remove its Microphone and Accessibility entries.

Windows Handy

  1. Quit Handy from the system tray (right-click → Quit).
  2. Settings → Apps → Installed apps → Handy → Uninstall.
  3. Delete the leftover data folder: press Win+R, type %APPDATA%, Enter, and delete the com.pais.handy folder (also check %LOCALAPPDATA% for one). The models folder inside is the bulk of it.

Space freed: roughly 1 to 2 GB with Parakeet only; several GB more if you added Whisper models.

12. FAQ

Is it really private? Yes. Transcription happens on your computer with a locally stored model. Both apps are open source, so this claim is publicly checkable in the code. The one exception is opt-in: VoiceInk's optional cloud clean-up sends transcribed text (never audio) to the provider you configure.

Does it work offline? Yes, fully, once the model has downloaded. Flight mode is fine.

Does it understand accents? Very well. Parakeet and Whisper are trained on hugely varied speech. Give it a few days and add your recurring names to the dictionary; accuracy settles above what you would guess.

Other languages? This guide assumes English. Parakeet V3 covers about 25 European languages; for broader coverage add a Whisper model (see section 8).

Does it type into any app? Effectively yes: anywhere a cursor accepts text. A handful of odd apps and secure fields (password boxes) refuse synthetic typing; the History view always has your text to paste manually.

Why not the built-in macOS/Windows dictation? The built-ins are okay for a sentence but weaker on accuracy and punctuation, historically cloud-linked for the best results, and awkward across apps. Local Parakeet-class models are simply a generation better, and everything stays on the machine.

Is it safe to click past the security warning? When, and only when, you downloaded the installer yourself from the official site named in this guide. The warnings mean "unknown publisher", not "malware found". Never click past that warning for something that arrived by email or a random link.

What does the VoiceInk licence cover? A one-time payment for the number of Macs in the tier ($29 one, $49 two, $69 three at the time of writing), all future updates included, 14-day money-back guarantee. Handy has no licence; it is free. There is also a free do-it-yourself route to VoiceInk for the technically inclined: section 15.

13. Glossary

ModelThe AI file that turns audio into text. Downloaded once, runs locally. Bigger is not automatically better; Parakeet is small and excellent.
ParakeetA fast, accurate speech model (from NVIDIA's research line) that runs well on ordinary CPUs and Apple chips. The default choice in this guide.
WhisperOpenAI's family of speech models. Strong multilingual coverage; the larger ones want a GPU.
Hotkey / shortcutThe key you hold or tap to record.
Push-to-talk vs toggleHold-to-record (release to finish) versus tap-to-start, tap-to-stop. Prefer push-to-talk: the mic is live only while you hold.
Accessibility permissionmacOS permission that lets an app type text into other apps on your behalf. Required for dictation to insert text.
GatekeepermacOS's check on downloaded apps. Unknown-publisher apps need a one-time "Open Anyway" in System Settings.
SmartScreenWindows's equivalent reputation check: the blue "Windows protected your PC" box, bypassed per-app with More info → Run anyway.

macOS 15. For tinkerers: build VoiceInk yourself, free

VoiceInk's source code is public under an open-source licence (GPLv3), and building it yourself is free and entirely legitimate: the developer publishes the instructions. This is the only route that is both free and VoiceInk. It is genuinely a developer-tools exercise, so it lives here at the back rather than in the main flow.

Is it for you? Only if you are comfortable typing commands in Terminal and can spare the setup: Xcode (Apple's free developer app, a very large download of tens of gigabytes), about 5 GB of working disk on top, and roughly 45 minutes the first time.

What you give up versus the paid build: no automatic updates (you re-run the build to update, and must never accept the update the app itself offers; see the warning below), no iCloud dictionary sync, no support, and first launch involves developer-style permission wrangling. Everything else in this guide works identically.

The shape of it (the door, not the full tour):

# One-off preparation
#   1. Install Xcode from the Mac App Store, open it once, accept the licence
#   2. Install the Metal Toolchain component:
xcodebuild -downloadComponent MetalToolchain

# Get the source and build
mkdir -p ~/Developer/voiceink-build && cd ~/Developer/voiceink-build
git clone https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk.git
cd VoiceInk
make local
# ~20 minutes later the app appears in ~/Downloads; drag it to Applications

# Before opening it, turn off the in-app update check (see the warning below)
defaults write com.prakashjoshipax.VoiceInk VoiceInkChecksForUpdatesOnLaunch -bool false
One caveat to know upfront

macOS ties the Accessibility permission to the app's signature, and home-built apps get a new signature on every rebuild, so after each update you re-grant Accessibility. There is a tidy fix (a one-off self-signed certificate), covered in the companion build guide below.

Never accept an update offered inside the app

A home-built VoiceInk still contains the developer's updater. If you accept an update from its update available button or from Check for Updates…, it replaces your build with the official paid version. That version has a different signature, so macOS stops honouring your permissions and dictation stops working, with the message that accessibility permissions are not provided. The command above turns the update check off; the update script in the companion guide keeps it off, and its troubleshooting section covers recovery if it has already happened.

The full walkthrough, with every command, every error met along the way and its fix, the certificate trick, and an update script, lives in a companion document: VoiceInk: Build and Setup Guide (published alongside this file). The official upstream instructions are in BUILDING.md on GitHub.
